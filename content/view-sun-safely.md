## Introduction to Safe Solar Viewing
The Sun is the star at the centre of our Solar System, a massive sphere of hot plasma [1]. Viewing the Sun directly can be dangerous, causing damage to your eyes. However, with the right equipment, you can safely observe the Sun.

## The Importance of Certified Filters
To view the Sun safely, you need a certified solar viewer or solar-film filter [2]. These filters have an optical density of 5, meaning they only permit 1 part in 100,000 of incident light to pass through. They also block ultraviolet and infrared rays, which can harm your eyes.

## Dangerous Shortcuts to Avoid
Do not use standard sunglasses or any other makeshift filters to view the Sun. These are not designed to block the intense radiation emitted by the Sun and can cause serious eye damage.

## Using Certified Solar Viewers
When using a certified solar viewer, make sure it is specifically designed for direct viewing of the Sun [2]. Look for the recommended optical density of 5 and ensure that the filter is not damaged or scratched.

## Practical Observing Tips
To observe the Sun safely, use your certified solar viewer or solar-film filter during the day, preferably when the Sun is not too high in the sky. This will reduce the amount of radiation that passes through the filter. Always supervise children when they are using solar viewers, and never look at the Sun without a certified filter.
